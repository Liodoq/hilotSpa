What happens when you come in

Our bone setter works by feel — finding what is tight, what has shifted, and what should be left alone. That takes a conversation first, and the conversation usually happens while you are already sore and half undressed.

So we moved it earlier. You mark where it hurts before you arrive, and your practitioner has read it by the time you sit down.

The words on our menu

What each one actually is, in plain terms.

Hilothee·lot
Hands-on work along the muscle and joint — pressing, warming and easing what has tightened. The oldest thing we do, and the base of most of the others.
Manghihilotmang·hee·hee·lot
The practitioner. Ours is a bone setter — taught by another bone setter, over years, the way the practice has always moved.
Ventosaven·toh·sa
Glass cups warmed and set on the back. As the air inside cools it draws the skin up, and the cup holds until it is lifted off. It leaves round marks that fade.
Suobsoo·ob
Herbal steam. You sit wrapped over a pot of boiled leaves and let the warmth do the work. Traditionally taken after a session rather than before.
Lanalah·na
Coconut oil, often steeped with ginger or local herbs. It is what our hands use; you will smell it before you feel it.

If a word on the menu is not here, ask the front desk — nobody minds explaining, and nobody should have to book something they cannot name.

Who decides what is safe for you

Our own bone setter decides which treatments suit which conditions, and writes it down. That list lives in this system as a plain table, and every suggestion you see has been checked against it first.

The software enforces the spa's rule. It does not author one.

If a treatment has been ruled out for something you told us about, it is filtered out before the assistant is asked anything at all — so it cannot suggest it by accident.

About the booking assistant

It suggests treatments and holds a time for you. It is not a doctor, and the lines below are limits rather than disclaimers — they are enforced in the system itself.

  • It offers only treatments this spa actually provides.
  • It cannot suggest anything our practitioner has ruled out for your condition.
  • It will not tell you what is wrong with you, or what will cure it.
  • If you describe something urgent, it asks you to see a physician.

Your record

Your assessments stay on your own account, so your practitioner can see whether something is easing between visits rather than starting from nothing each time. After a session you can say how it feels now — that is the only way the spa learns whether the work helped.

We do not share your details.
